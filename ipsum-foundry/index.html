What is Lorem Ipsum?

The short version

Lorem Ipsum is dummy text: scrambled, meaningless pseudo-Latin used to fill a layout before real content exists. It's been the standard placeholder in printing and design for so long that the opening words — "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet…" — are instantly recognizable even though the phrase itself means nothing.

Why use nonsense at all?

Because readable text is distracting. Drop in a real sentence and people start reading it, judging it, editing it — instead of looking at the typeface, the spacing, the layout, which is the actual thing being tested. Gibberish with the right rhythm and letter distribution lets a design get judged as a design.

Where it actually comes from

It's not made-up Latin — it's real Latin, badly mangled. The source is a passage from Cicero's De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum ("On the Ends of Good and Evil"), written in 45 BC, a philosophical dialogue about the nature of pleasure and pain. At some point — the popular story points to a 16th-century typesetter scrambling a galley of type to make a specimen sheet — words got cut, reordered, and Latin-ified further, and the resulting nonsense stuck around and outlived whatever the typesetter was actually trying to sell. It got a second life in the 1960s on Letraset dry-transfer sheets (rub-on lettering graphic designers used before computers), and again in the 1980s–90s when it shipped as the default filler in desktop publishing software like Aldus PageMaker — which is how it ended up as the universal default it is today.

Is it actually Latin, though?

Sort of. Every individual word traces back to real Latin, but the sentences don't mean anything and wouldn't parse to a Latin speaker. "Lorem" itself isn't even a real Latin word on its own — it's a fragment sliced out of the middle of "dolorem" (a form of "pain, sorrow"), leaving a chunk that looks and sounds like a word but was never meant to stand alone. The whole passage is best thought of as Latin-flavored static: real letters, real word roots, zero grammar or meaning.

How much should you actually use?

Only as much as the layout needs, and treat it as temporary. Filler text is for judging proportions, line length, and hierarchy — not for testing how a design handles real edge cases (long headlines, short headlines, a name with no vowels, an empty state). Swap in real content as early as possible, since dummy text has a well-known habit of accidentally surviving into production if nobody remembers to replace it.
